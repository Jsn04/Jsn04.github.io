Driver Drowsiness Detection

Cross-dataset evaluation of CNN and CNN-LSTM models for visual driver drowsiness detection. The core research question: can models trained on one dataset actually generalise to another? The answer, under subject-disjoint splits that prevent identity leakage, is not as clean as within-dataset numbers suggest. This work validated that finding and proposed a stricter evaluation protocol for the field.

Motivation

The Evaluation Problem

Most drowsiness detection papers report accuracy on within-dataset splits. The problem: the same driver appears in both train and test. The model can learn identity cues — the shape of a specific person's face, blinking pattern — instead of learning generalised drowsiness indicators. This inflates reported performance significantly.

Subject-disjoint evaluation is the stricter test: train and test splits contain different people entirely. This forces the model to learn features that transfer across individuals. The accuracy gap between in-subject and cross-subject evaluation is the real measure of whether a model is practically useful.

Standard eval (weak)
Within-dataset split

Same subjects in train and test. Model can learn identity cues. Reports high accuracy but doesn't demonstrate generalisation. Most published numbers are this.

This paper's eval (strict)
Subject-disjoint split

Different subjects in train and test. Identity leakage is impossible. Accuracy drops — but it reflects real-world deployment performance more honestly.

Method

Detection Pipeline

01
Face + landmark detection

dlib 68-point facial landmark detector localises eye regions frame-by-frame. Eye Aspect Ratio (EAR) computed from the landmark coordinates as a baseline signal.

02
Eye region extraction

Cropped and normalised eye patches extracted per frame. Consistent sizing and contrast normalisation applied before feeding into the neural models.

03
CNN — spatial features

Convolutional network trained on the eye patch to detect closed/drowsy states from a single frame. Strong at per-frame classification, blind to temporal patterns.

04
CNN-LSTM — temporal modelling

CNN feature extractor feeds an LSTM layer that models blink patterns and gradual eyelid closure over a window of frames. Captures the temporal signature of drowsiness that single-frame models miss.

05
Cross-dataset evaluation

Both models trained on one dataset (with subject-disjoint train/val), tested on a second dataset with entirely different subjects and recording conditions. Performance delta quantifies the generalisation gap.

Results

Key Findings

CNN-LSTM consistently outperformed the pure CNN under cross-subject and cross-dataset conditions, confirming that temporal modelling captures generalisation-relevant features that spatial-only models do not. The accuracy gap between in-subject and cross-subject evaluation was substantial — validating the core concern about published benchmarks.
